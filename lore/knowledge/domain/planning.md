# Planning bounded context

`reeve-planning` owns the work hierarchy, the delivery gate, required decisions, pull-request links, sprints and goals, urgent marks, promised-by dates, item links, web links and brief templates. How its crate is laid out (ports, adapters, transactions, clock) is in [[general/architecture]]; this node says what the context means and what it will not do. Reason: an agent changing planning needs the language and the guarantees before the file layout.

## Ubiquitous language

The words below are bound across API, database, CLI and UI (kb/ADR-0002 R6; sprint, goal, confidence, verdict and upkeep are trial names under kb/ADR-0014 R6; urgent is added by kb/ADR-0017; attachment by kb/ADR-0022; promise, overdue and late by kb/ADR-0025; item link and web link by kb/ADR-0031; brief template by the observation of 2026-11-25 on kb/ADR-0002). In the decision queue, an *affected* story is an unfinished story whose readiness reasons name the decision; it is *held* when todo and *under way* when started. `needed_in` (this sprint, next sprint, beyond, none) says when a decision is needed, the *next step* what it needs, and the *lane* where the queue lists it; all three are derived on read and never stored (kb/ADR-0014 R6, kb/ADR-0027 R1). A *discovery row* is an initiative or epic without its discovery mark that holds unfinished stories; it is the *discovery item* of each of those stories, the nearest unmarked item above it that the story's readiness names. Rows and decisions share one set of lanes; a row is only ever in the two sprint lanes or Now (see *The readiness queue*).

- **Initiative, epic, story.** The three levels of the hierarchy; only stories are delivered. Keys come from one sequence (`ABC-12`, kb/ADR-0002 R5). An initiative has a **horizon** (a configured bucket) and an optional target month.
- **Kind.** A story is a `story`, a `bug` or a `chore`. A story of kind `story` needs an epic; an **untriaged** story is one of kind `story` with no epic (`Story::is_untriaged`).
- **Delivery state and resolution.** `todo`, `in_progress`, `in_review`, `testing`, `done`; a story that enters `done` carries a **resolution**, `completed` or `dropped`.
- **Blocker.** A story that blocks another until it is merged (testing or done); a started one never blocks its own owner's dependents (kb/ADR-0027 R5).
- **Readiness.** A story is ready or it has reasons; computed on read, never stored (kb/ADR-0027 R1). A **discovery mark** on an initiative or epic says its discovery is done.
- **Override.** An interactive caller's written reason for entering active work although the story is not ready (kb/ADR-0027 R2). **Grandfathered** stories are imported already in their state and are never overrides.
- **Required decision.** A decision a work item waits on, with a **decider**, a due date and an optional **reversibility** (`one_way` or `two_way`, kb/ADR-0013). It points at a **record reference** (repository, path, record id) in a household repository. Its **check result** is `unchecked`, `accepted`, `not_accepted`, `proposed_pr_open` or `not_found`. It ends with a **closure kind**, `decided` or `withdrawn`.
- **Sprint, goal, goal link, upkeep line.** A sprint is a draft (the next sprint: no window, at most one), active (a window of time from its opening) or closed; a goal is a team intention with confidence and verdict, a goal link ties a goal to an initiative, epic or story (`link_goal`; `sprint::serves` says which goals a story serves, through itself, its epic or its epic's initiative; kb/ADR-0014 R3), and the upkeep line is the configured reference for how much of a sprint is upkeep (kb/ADR-0014). A *candidate* is a draft's goal: a role in conversation and in kb/ADR-0026, not bound vocabulary; the API and the UI say goal.
- **Urgent mark, urgent, war room.** A person marks one initiative, epic or story **urgent**, with an optional reason; the mark lasts one sprint length unless renewed, and marking again replaces it. While a mark is active its item, the item's ancestors and its descendants are urgent (a done story is not, and a mark on a done story makes nothing urgent); urgency is computed on read, never stored. The **war room** lists every stored mark, active then expired, until a person clears or renews it (kb/ADR-0017).
- **Attachment.** An image (PNG, JPEG or WebP) owned by one initiative, epic or story, shown inline in its Markdown and listed under the item. It is uploaded first and owned by none until the write that saves the text showing it claims it (kb/ADR-0022 R2); only its uploader sees it until then. The UI says image for what a person adds and attachment for what an item owns; it never says unclaimed.
- **Promise, effective date, holder, state, overdue, late, due.** A person **promises** a story or an epic by a calendar date, with an optional short recipient; initiatives are not promised and agents cannot promise (kb/ADR-0025 R1, R2). A story's **effective date** is the earlier of its own promise and the one it gets from its epic, which applies to each story not done when it was set (R3); the **holder** says whose date it is: the story or the epic. A story's promise **state** is derived on read from the effective date, its delivery and the request's UTC date: `open`; `overdue` (unfinished after the date, and it keeps its date until a person changes it); `kept` (done on or before the date); `late` (done after it); `dropped` (resolved dropped); an epic has no state of its own and a done story without `done_at` has none (R4). An open promise is **due** `this_sprint`, `next_sprint` or `later` by one function over the active and draft sprints' planned `ends_on` (R5). An epic's own promise is **past** once its date is before today: a word for display (the epic chip's "Past 20 Nov", shown with the overdue tone and clock; `past` on the wire), not a state; R4 gives an epic none. **A sprint's promise range** is R8's: the dates after the previous sprint's planned `ends_on` and up to its own (`sprint_range`; `promises_after` on the wire; a draft has none), and a sprint's promises are the stories whose effective date is in it. "Window" stays with a sprint's opening-to-closing span and with the decision queue: `promise::window` (and `PromiseWindow`, `StoryPromise::window`) is the window a promise asks a blocking decision for, as `queue.rs` calls a story's `NeededIn` its window; it was renamed from `lane` so as not to collide with `queue::lane`. Nothing is stored but the date, who set it, when and the recipient. There is no "slipped" and no "broken".
- **Item link.** A named, informational connection between two different items of any level: `follows` (read from the other item as "is followed by"), `duplicates` ("is duplicated by") or `relates_to` (the same from both sides, stored once). The name is `type` on the wire, `link_type` in the `item_links` table, and `ItemLinkType` in code. It holds nothing back: the blocker is the only relation the gate reads. Say "item link"; bare "link" already means a goal link, a pull-request link and a decision's record link, so bare "link" stays where no goal link, pull-request link or decision's record link is in view (kb/ADR-0031 R6).
- **Web link.** A title and an `http` or `https` URL on one item of any level, in a list of its own beside the item links, oldest first. The title is stored trimmed, 1 to 200 characters on one line, none of them a control character (`WebLink::new`). It belongs to its one item and is part of nothing else; like an item link it takes and changes no version and holds nothing back. The stored URL is the URL parser's serialisation, and two URLs are the same when those are equal; an item has a URL once, and a web link is not edited in place (remove it, add it again). A URL with a user name or a password is refused, because both activity entries keep the URL (kb/ADR-0031 R5).
- **Brief template.** A named Markdown text an admin keeps for one level, `initiative` or `epic` (`BriefTemplateLevel`; a story has none), from which a new brief can be prefilled. Its name is stored trimmed, 1 to 100 characters on one line, none of them a control character, and is unique at its level, compared by a key the server computes: the name trimmed and lowercased (`name_key`, `str::to_lowercase`, never the database's `lower()`). Its text is kept exactly as written; it must not be blank and must hold no NUL character. At most one template per level carries the **default** mark. Every member reads the templates; only an admin creates, edits, removes or marks one. It only prefills, and only when the create asks (by name, or for the level's default): the item keeps no reference to it, nothing is validated against it, and editing or removing it changes no brief. Say "brief template"; bare "template" is used only in `reeve template`, `--template` and `--no-template`, and the create fields `template` and `default_template`. One other thing carries the name and is not one: Lorekeeper's planning skills lay briefs out by a reference file of their own, `reference/brief-template.md`. That file is the layout a skill writes by, kept in Lorekeeper, not a text an admin keeps in the planner. Renaming it there (for example `brief-layout.md`) is a follow-up; until then call it "the skill's brief layout", so that "brief template" means one thing.
- **Due this sprint and a sprint's promise range differ.** R5's "due this sprint" has no lower bound (any open date up to the active sprint's planned end), while R8's range starts after the previous sprint's planned end. They agree while sprints close on their planned ends; when a sprint closes on or before its planned end, an open promise dated up to that end still reads "· this sprint" and matches `promised=this_sprint`, while only the closed sprint lists it. Both follow kb/ADR-0025 as written; which one the active sprint's list shows is settled in GRA-648 (GRA-610's sprint page shows no promises), before `GET /sprints` fills it.
- **Client affordances that copy a server rule on purpose.** The promise dialog refuses a past date before sending, offers a sprint shortcut only while that sprint's planned end is not past, limits the recipient to 200 characters (counted in code points, as the server counts), and notes when the epic's promise is earlier (`epicNote`, its words pinned in `promise.fixture.json`); the board filters stories by `promised` on the client with `promisedMatches`, whose truth table is that fixture's `filter` rows, which `Filter::matches`'s test reads too. Each is a UX affordance so the person sees the problem before submitting; the server re-checks every one (`change`, `shortcut`, `MAX_RECIPIENT`, `story_promise`), and its answer wins. One copy in the CLI has no server re-check: `same_url` (`crates/reeve/src/relations.rs`) parses a typed URL the way the server stores one, so that `reeve link rm <KEY> --url` finds the web link whatever spelling was typed, and the removal that follows is by the id the server listed for it, a request that carries no URL. If the copy ever disagrees with the server's form, it finds nothing and removes nothing (the command says so and lists the item's web links); it can never remove a different web link, since a match is equality with a stored URL and the delete is by that row's id. A shortcut is sent only while the end it filled is still the end offered for it; once the end moves or the sprint closes under the open dialog, the date in the field is sent instead. The Relations component (`web/src/lib/relations.ts`, kb/ADR-0031) copies the following the same way: the forward wording of a name and the display order of the names are copied from the protocol's table (`linkTypeText`, `LINK_TYPES`; the wording of a listed item link is the server's `label`, never the client's; nothing holds `linkTypeText` equal to the protocol's `out` wording except the web smoke, for `follows`); the informational notice after a `follows` between two stories is decided separately in the web client (`followsNote`) and twice in the CLI: `reeve link add` on adding, and `reeve apply` whenever the answer holds one, created or existing; each client words its own second sentence; an item link read from its `in` side is removed with the two keys swapped (`removal`), because writes exist only in the forward form; the Add form's hints restate kb/ADR-0031 R1's meanings of `follows` and `duplicates` (`linkTypeHint`); the item search leaves the item itself out of its hits (`ItemSearch.svelte`); `webHost` repeats kb/ADR-0031 R5's http/https rule to decide whether a stored URL becomes a live link; and the web link form refuses an empty trimmed title or URL before sending. Of these the server re-checks the self link (it refuses one), the name sent, which must be one it knows, and the web link's title and URL; `webHost` reads what the server already stored and has nothing to send back; the wording, the notice and the hints are text with nothing to re-check, and a removal sent with the keys unswapped is answered ok and removes nothing, or, where the opposite item link between the two also exists, removes that one instead; the web smoke holds the swap by reading the other item's links afterwards.
- **Client affordances of brief templates (web only).** The web create forms for an initiative and an epic show a "Template" select (`TemplatePicker.svelte`, the rule in `web/src/lib/templates.ts`) whenever the level has a template. When its list arrives the level's default is preselected and its text is put into the brief, once per draft and only while the brief is blank: a new initiative draft that was cancelled and reopened keeps its brief, an emptied one included, and an epic form starts a new draft after each create. Choosing another entry replaces the brief only while it is untouched, that is blank or still exactly the text the picker last put there; once the author has changed one character, the form asks instead ("Replace" or "Keep my text") and never replaces the text without that answer. What the picker last put there is kept with the draft (an initiative draft keeps it across Cancel and reopen, and loses it with the draft after a create); it is never inferred from a brief that happens to equal a listed template's body, which may be the author's own typing. A reopened draft shows the entry last applied only while the brief is still exactly its text, and "No template" otherwise. "No template" empties an untouched prefill the same way, and asks ("Remove my text") otherwise. The forms are not sent while the list is loading (for at most ten seconds; after that, or when it fails, the author writes the brief alone), and they send only `brief` (null when blank), never `template` or `default_template`, so what is created is exactly the text the author saw and `starting_brief` takes it as the brief. None of this copies a server rule: the CLI and the API apply a template only when the create asks, as *Which brief a new initiative or epic gets* says. The templates page (`web/src/routes/(account)/templates/+page.svelte`, under the account menu) lists every template for every member; only an admin sees its controls (create, edit, remove, the default mark), and the server decides each write (`require_admin`). An edit sends only the fields changed since the editor opened, under the version it read; a conflict keeps the admin's changed fields over the reloaded template, and a refusal is shown in the server's words in the form that was saved. The edit form refuses a blank name or text before sending, a copy of `BriefTemplate::new`'s rule that the server re-checks.

## Aggregates

Each aggregate is a Rust type under `crates/reeve-planning/src/domain/`, persisted by one port in `src/ports/`. Rules live on the aggregate or beside it in `domain/`; the handler loads, calls, persists and writes the activity entry. Reason: a rule on the aggregate is testable without a database. For moves and decisions the aggregate is the only way in (private fields, `Story::move_to`, `RequiredDecision`'s methods), so a second caller cannot skip the rule. Item edits are not there yet: `edit_story` checks the title (`check_title`) and the criteria in the handler, `edit_epic` and `edit_initiative` validate through `Epic::new` and `Initiative::new`, and all three rebuild the item with `from_persisted` before `update_*` writes it, so a new edit path must repeat those checks.

| Aggregate | Owns | Port |
|---|---|---|
| `Initiative` | title, brief, horizon, target month, discovery mark | `InitiativeRepo` |
| `Epic` | title, brief, parent initiative, discovery mark | `EpicRepo` |
| `Story` | kind, description, criteria, owner, labels, rank, delivery, resolution | `StoryRepo` |
| `RequiredDecision` | planner fields, record reference, PR url, check, closure | `DecisionRepo` |
| `Sprint` | window and state | `SprintRepo` |
| `Goal` | outcome, check, owner, notes, confidence and verdict, and its sprint, changed only by an opening | `SprintRepo` |
| `UrgentMark` | time, person and optional reason of the mark on one item | `UrgencyRepo` |
| `Promise` (not part of `Story` or `Epic`) | date, setter, time and optional recipient on one story or epic | `PromiseRepo` |
| `ItemLink` (an edge, part of no item) | the two keys and the name | `ItemLinkRepo` |
| `WebLink` (on one item, part of none) | title and URL | `WebLinkRepo` |
| `BriefTemplate` (names no item, and no item names it) | level, name and text; the default mark and the version are on its stored row | `BriefTemplateRepo` |
| `Attachment` | owner (one item, or none), uploader, filename, type, size and hash; its bytes as 1 MiB chunks | `AttachmentRepo` |

`Sprint` and `Goal` are two aggregates behind one port: each has its own version and is locked (`sprint_for_update`, `goal_for_update`) and saved (`save_sprint`, `save_goal`) on its own. A `GoalLink` (goal id and item key) is a row of its own, written by `insert_goal_link` and `delete_goal_link`, not a field of `Goal`. Blocking edges are not part of `Story`: they are rows written by `StoryRepo::add_block` and `remove_block` and read as readiness input (`ReadinessInput::blockers`, each with its delivery and owner, plus the story's `owner`; `domain/readiness.rs`'s `blocks` decides which count, kb/ADR-0027 R5). Comments have no domain type: `CommentRepo` inserts them (`insert_comment`, `insert_item_comment`) and returns `CommentDto` rows. The "no DTO" guarantee below covers `domain/` only: some ports still carry wire types (`CommentRepo`'s `CommentDto`, `GoalVia` in `PlanningReads::active_goals_of_stories`). A pull request (`domain/pull_request.rs`, `PullRequestRepo`) is a cache of GitHub's state, not an aggregate with business rules. An urgent mark is stored in its item's row (`urgent_at`, `urgent_by`, `urgent_reason`) but is no field of `Initiative`, `Epic` or `Story`: only `UrgencyRepo` reads or writes those columns, and no item `UPDATE` names them, so marking takes no version and no edit or move drops a mark (kb/ADR-0017 R1, R5). A promise sits beside `Story` and `Epic` the same way: four columns on the item's row, no field of either aggregate, written only through `PromiseRepo` (`set_promise`, `clear_promise`, after `lock_for_promise`'s `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`) without taking or changing the item's version. Its rules are pure and live in `domain/promise.rs`. An item link is the same kind of thing as a goal link and a blocking edge: a row of its own, owned by neither item, which is why adding one changes no item's version and is logged on both items (`ItemLink`, `domain/item_link.rs`: no link from an item to itself, and `relates_to` stored with the lower key, in byte order, first).

### Invariants and where they live

- **The delivery rule is `Story::move_to`** (`domain/items.rs`): entering `done` needs a resolution and a completed story needs an active origin, re-resolving a done story follows the same rule (kb/ADR-0002 R4), a not-ready entry into active work is refused, and an override needs an interactive caller and a non-blank reason (kb/ADR-0027 R2). It returns `Moved::Unchanged` or `Moved::Moved { from, override_reason }`, or a `MoveRefused`, and leaves the story untouched on a refusal. It also sets the owner when a story without one enters any active state (kb/ADR-0027 R6); the gate judges such a story as the mover's (`readiness_in`'s `judge_as`). It receives `ready: bool` and nothing from sprints or goals. Reason: readiness is a derived fact the handler loads (kb/ADR-0027 R1), and a goal must never decide a move (kb/ADR-0014 R4).
- **Decision rules are `RequiredDecision::{link, edit, record_check, close}`** (`domain/decision.rs`), all refusing with `DecisionError` and leaving the decision untouched. `link` resets the check to `unchecked` (kb/ADR-0004 R3); `edit` changes only title, decider, due and reversibility and returns the typed `FieldChange`s; `record_check` refuses, in order, no reference, a result for a changed reference (a conflict at the API), and a closed decision, and reports `CheckOutcome::Unchanged` for a result that says the same; `close` needs a reason, and a one-way decision cannot be closed as `decided` (`OneWayDecided`). Input is validated by value objects built before any row is read: `RecordRef::new`, `PrUrl::parse`, `DecisionEdit::new`.
- **A changed horizon must be a configured bucket** (`check_horizon_change`, `domain/items.rs`); an unchanged horizon is always valid, so unlisting a bucket does not block editing the initiative's other fields. Create and import check the bucket in `Initiative::new`.
- **An initiative's owner that a request names must be an allowed user, and a target month that a request names must be `YYYY-MM`** with a month 01 to 12. Both are checked in the server's shared actions (`actions::allowed_owner`, `actions::named_target_month`), not in the aggregate: who is allowed is identity's to say, and the shape of a month is `reeve_protocol::is_target_month`, which the Now ranking reads stored months with. Like the horizon, each is judged only when the request names it: a stored owner who is no longer allowed and a stored month in another shape are always valid to keep, so neither blocks editing the initiative's other fields, and either can be cleared. Import and plan apply do not go through the shared actions and run neither check: an imported initiative keeps the owner and the month the other tool had. Reason: an owner nobody can sign in as holds work no one can pick up, and a month in another shape silently counts as none in the ranking.
- **Brief template rules: the name and the text are `BriefTemplate::new`; one name per level and one default per level are the handler's, under one lock** (`domain/brief_template.rs`, `handler/brief_templates.rs`). `BriefTemplate::new` refuses a name that is not 1 to 100 characters on one line after trimming (`BriefTemplateError::Name`), a blank text (`Blank`) and a text with a NUL character (`Nul`), each with its own message. Every template write first takes the `reeve_brief_templates` advisory lock (`lock_brief_templates`), reads the rows and decides on them: a name another template of the level has, compared by `name_key`, is refused with a message that names that template's id, and marking a default takes the mark off the level's previous one in the same transaction. The table backs both: a unique constraint on `(level, name_key)` and a partial unique index on `level` where `is_default` (migration 1015). An edit takes the version the caller read and answers Conflict with the current one otherwise; an edit that changes nothing writes nothing; the level never changes. Only an edit changes the version: the default mark takes none and changes none, and a removal takes none. A create is idempotent by content (level, name, text), which is stronger than a key for a load from files: the same three again are answered with the stored template and write nothing, and the same name with another text is the name refusal. Each write records one activity entry in its transaction, on the entity `brief_template` keyed by the template's id: `brief_template_created`, `brief_template_edited`, `brief_template_removed`, `brief_template_default_set` and `brief_template_default_cleared`. Every payload holds `level` and `name`; an edit adds `fields` (which of `name` and `body` changed) and, on a rename, `from` (the old name); a set default adds `replaced` (the id of the template that had the mark, or null); a removal adds `was_default`. No entry holds the text, and because the log is append-only a template's name stays in it after the template is removed. Tests: those in `domain/brief_template.rs`; `brief_template_writes_log_who_changed_what_and_never_the_body`, `brief_template_names_and_the_default_are_one_per_level`, `a_brief_template_and_its_entry_commit_together` and `overlapping_brief_template_writes_are_serialised` in `crates/reeve-planning/tests/handlers.rs`; `brief_templates_are_stored_per_level_under_a_unique_name_key` and `the_brief_template_lock_holds_a_second_writer_until_the_first_ends` in `crates/reeve-planning/tests/repo.rs`.
- **Which brief a new initiative or epic gets is decided in the server's actions, not here** (`actions/templates.rs::starting_brief`, called by `actions::create_initiative` and `create_epic`), and a template is applied only when the caller asks: a brief that is not blank is the brief; else `template` names the level's template; else `default_template: true` gives the level's default (none marked: an empty brief); else the brief is what was sent. `template` together with `default_template`, and a brief that is not blank together with either, are refused, as is a name no template of the level has, unless the create's idempotency key already made an item of the level: a retry is answered that item whatever happened to the template since (`Handlers::made_under` is the lookup, and it runs what the create handlers check before their own lookup, the attachment limit, through the same `before_create`, so it refuses what their replay refuses). Planning's create handlers take the finished text and know nothing of templates, which is why plan apply and the import, which call them directly, never apply one; all planning offers a create is `Handlers::brief_template_body` and, for that retry, `Handlers::made_under`. Two advisory findings see a template's text as the brief it has become, and both are accepted: a brief prefilled from a template and left unfilled is not empty to `empty_brief` (`a_brief_started_from_a_template_is_not_found_empty` in `crates/reeve-server/tests/templates.rs`), and a template whose text says "decided", "Jira" or "Confluence" (or links to an Atlassian page) raises `brief_cites_without_decision` on an item made from it that has no required decision on it or above it. Test: `a_new_brief_starts_from_a_template_only_when_the_create_asks` in the same file.
- **A sprint allows a write only in some states** (`sprint::allow` over `Op`, `domain/sprint.rs`): one explicit table, so a new state or operation fails to compile (kb/ADR-0014 R1, R6).
- **Urgency is `urgency` over the stored marks and their `Lineage`** (`domain/urgency.rs`): a mark is active until `expires_at`, its time plus the configured sprint length (kb/ADR-0017 R2); an active mark on a story reaches its epic and initiative, on an epic its initiative and its unfinished stories, on an initiative its epics and their unfinished stories, never siblings, and never further from a derived urgency (R3). The handler computes it once per snapshot (`urgency_in`, `handler/urgent.rs`) and every item DTO carries it; nothing stores it (R1). Marking, renewing and clearing need an interactive caller (`interactive`, `handler/mod.rs`), are logged `urgent_marked` and `urgent_cleared`, never `override` (R4), and a reason is trimmed and at most 200 characters (`clean_reason`). The rules on the mark itself are still in the handler and the adapter's SQL; they move onto the domain when next touched.
- **Attachment rules are pure** (`domain/attachment.rs`): `ImageType::sniff` names the type by the bytes, `strip_metadata` removes EXIF, XMP, IPTC, comments, MPF, JFIF thumbnails, trailers, PNG text and what animation frames carry besides their bitstream, without touching image data, and keeps only a non-upright orientation; `clean_filename` and the 10 MiB cap (kb/ADR-0023 R1–R3). A person may hold at most 200 MiB of unclaimed uploads (`MAX_UNCLAIMED_BYTES`). The claim is one conditional `UPDATE` in the adapter, inside the saving write's transaction (`claim_attachments`, `handler/attachments.rs`): the caller's unclaimed uploads become the item's, ones already the item's and ones gone are ignored, anything else refuses the whole write. An upload, a claim and a delete never take or change the item's version; `attachment_added` and `attachment_removed` are logged on the item, and an unclaimed upload is never logged (kb/ADR-0023 R7).
- **Promise rules are pure** (`domain/promise.rs`): the effective date, the state, the due period and a sprint's promise range are functions of the stored facts and a `Clock` (today and the active and draft sprints' planned `ends_on`). `Clock::at(now, sprints)` is its one assembly: `promise_clock_in` (`handler/promise.rs`) builds it once per read snapshot from `SprintRepo::active_and_draft_in`, and the sprint views from the sprints they have already read; it travels beside the urgency into the story and epic assemblers, and every view, filter and queue rule classifies through it (kb/ADR-0025 R4, R5, R8). `StoryPromise` (private fields; `story_promise` is its only constructor) says whose promise holds as `HeldBy` (`Own` or `Epic(key)`), with that promise always present. Who may be promised is `Promisable` (`Story(delivery)` or `Epic`; never an initiative, R1): `change` takes it, refuses a done story (R2), and returns None when nothing changes (the same date and recipient); the port takes `PromiseItem` (`Story` or `Epic`), and the handler refuses an initiative before touching it. `sprint_range` selects R8's range, the draft rules included. The decision queue reads the domain value (`PromiseWindow::of`), never the wire DTO. Setting, changing and clearing need an interactive caller and are logged under their own actions, `promised` and `promise_cleared`, never `override` (R2).
- **Readiness is `compute_readiness` over a `ReadinessInput`** (`domain/readiness.rs`). The only derivation stored is a decision's check result, because it needs git.
- **The time facts `started_at` and `done_at` are set by `set_delivery`'s SQL** from the row's state, not by the aggregate. This is the current implementation choice, not a limit of the aggregate: `move_story` reads the story `FOR UPDATE` (`get_story_for_update`) before it calls `move_to`, so the story it holds has the locked state. The same SQL keeps a stored owner (`COALESCE(owner, …)`), so an owner change must not rely on `set_delivery`.
- **Fields are private.** `Story`, `RequiredDecision` and `RecordRef` expose getters and `from_persisted` for the adapter and the integration tests. Reason: a field that can be assigned bypasses the rule that guards it. `from_persisted` does not validate, so it is still a way around the rules: the item edit handlers use it, as above.
- **A plan (`reeve apply`, `apply_plan` in `handler/plans.rs`) is checked whole, then written piece by piece, and of its lists that connect items, only `blocks` gates.** A plan has three lists that connect items, and they are three different things. `blocks` (`{story, blocked_by}`) writes blocking edges between stories and is the only one of the three that gates (kb/ADR-0027); a plan's required decisions connect no two items and gate through readiness as any do. `links` (`{from, type, to}`; `from` follows, duplicates or relates to `to`) are item links: informational, between items of any level, and they hold nothing back. `web_links` (`{on, title, url}`) are web links. A plan written from another tool's data puts a blocking relation under `blocks` or leaves it out, never under `links` (kb/ADR-0031 R2); the CLI answers a blocking word written as a link's `type` with a pointer to `blocks`, from the one word list it shares with the name parser of `reeve link` and with the MCP item link tools (`is_blocking_word`, `crates/reeve-protocol/src/links.rs`); the pointer says that blockers are for stories and that a blocking relation between anything else is left out. A pointer exists in the CLI and in the two MCP item link tools: `add_item_link` and `remove_item_link` answer one of those words sent as `type` with "Blocking is not an item link. Do not add it under another name: tell the person that a blocker is added by a person, in the CLI or the web client." (`item_link_arguments` in `crates/reeve-server/src/mcp/tools.rs`). A direct API caller gets the closed enum's refusal without one. A plan's `type` uses the wire spelling (`relates_to`), while `reeve link add` takes `relates-to`. Each end of a link, and a web link's `on`, is a plan item of any level by name or an existing item by key or external reference, resolved as a decision's `on` is: the untyped resolution (`resolve_any`, native keys of any level before external references), which is what `reeve link add` uses. A reference is resolved in the scope of the place it stands in, and the answers are kept apart (`Refs`): a blocker's two stories, a story's `epic` and an epic's `initiative` take one level and are resolved for that level, so `OLD-1` under `blocks` may name the story that has it as its external reference while the same spelling at a link's end names the epic that has it as its key; neither answer is served to the other scope, in the check or in the writes. The plan's check reports together, before anything is written: more than 500 item links or more than 500 web links (`MAX_PLAN_ITEM_LINKS` and `MAX_PLAN_WEB_LINKS` in `reeve-protocol`: a plan is one request, answered inside the client's timeout), a name that an earlier apply of this plan id created at another level (`check_name_levels`: a name keeps its level across applies, because an item is found again by its level, the plan id and its name, and a name moved to another list would otherwise create a second item and take the plan's links with it), an end that names nothing, a link from an item to itself, a bad title or URL (`WebLink::new`, so a URL with a user name or a password too), one URL under two titles on one item within the plan, and a URL the item already has under another title. The level check has a known limit: two simultaneous applies of one plan id that give a name different levels can both pass it (tracked as GRA-888). Both URL checks ask `WebLink::is_new_among` (`check_plan_web_links`): once with the web links the plan's earlier entries give the item, where the refusal is worded as the plan's own (nothing is stored yet to remove), and once with the item's stored web links (read in one statement and grouped by item, so an entry is compared with its own item's web links only), where the method's wording stands. "One item" is judged on what a reference names, not on how it is spelled: each reference is resolved once (`plan_identities`) to a key, an existing item's or the one an earlier apply of this plan gave the name, or to the plan name itself while the item has no key; a key and a name are never one item. Two ends that do not exist yet are therefore compared by plan name; when the link is written both have keys, and the domain value judges them again (`ResolvedItemLink::new`, through `ItemLink::new`). So the plan name of an item that has a key, that key and its external reference are one item, in the self-link check (where `ItemLink::new` judges two keys) and in both URL checks. An item link name outside the three is not among those problems: the name is a closed enum on the wire, so the body is refused as it is read and that one problem is reported alone. Nothing wraps a plan in a transaction: each create commits on its own, then each blocker, then the item links and web links (`apply_plan_links`). Before that last phase the item links and web links the plan's items have today are read once, in one read-only snapshot, through the ports' reads a listing uses (`plan_links_today`: `item_links_of_in`, `web_links_of_in`); an entry found there is answered `created` false with no transaction, so a replay of a large plan costs two reads and not a transaction per entry. Every other entry gets a transaction of its own (begin, `add_item_link_in` with a `ResolvedItemLink` built from the ends the plan resolved, or `add_web_link_in`, commit), and the `_in` form's own idempotency covers an entry written after the read or listed twice in the plan. A web link's answer carries its id, which is how one is removed, whether this apply created it or it was there. So a written link's row and its entries on every item it names commit together (kb/ADR-0031 R7), and the entry is the last write before a commit ([[general/architecture]]). Never one transaction for all of a plan's links: it would hold the activity log's lock across a later insert and could deadlock with a concurrent `reeve link add` of the same link. A link that fails leaves the earlier ones in; every write answers `created` false when it was already there, so applying the same plan id again finishes, once whatever made the link fail is corrected (a web link retitled meanwhile is then reported by the check), and a repeat of a finished plan writes nothing. Tests: `a_plan_links_items_of_any_level_and_reapplying_it_creates_nothing`, `a_plan_with_a_bad_link_writes_nothing_and_names_every_problem`, `a_reapplied_plan_that_links_an_item_to_itself_under_two_spellings_writes_nothing`, `a_plan_attaches_web_links_and_reapplying_it_creates_nothing`, `a_plan_with_a_bad_web_link_writes_nothing_and_names_every_problem`, `a_plan_edited_to_retitle_a_web_link_is_refused_before_anything_is_written`, `a_plan_web_link_on_one_item_under_two_spellings_cannot_have_two_titles`, `a_plan_follows_between_two_stories_blocks_nothing_and_leaves_readiness_alone`, `a_plan_name_moved_to_another_level_is_refused_before_anything_is_written` and `a_plan_holds_at_most_500_item_links_and_500_web_links` in `crates/reeve-server/tests/plans.rs`; `a_plan_link_whose_entry_is_refused_is_left_out_and_the_earlier_links_stay`, `a_plan_web_link_whose_entry_is_refused_is_left_out_and_the_earlier_web_link_stays`, `concurrent_applies_of_one_plan_report_each_link_created_once`, `a_plan_reference_that_is_a_native_key_and_an_external_reference_names_each_in_its_place`, `a_plan_decision_on_a_native_story_key_that_is_an_epic_external_reference_is_on_the_story` and `a_replayed_plan_reads_its_links_once_and_sends_no_insert_for_one_that_exists` in `crates/reeve-planning/tests/handlers.rs`; `apply_points_a_blocking_word_under_links_at_the_blocks_list_and_sends_nothing` in `crates/reeve/tests/cli.rs`.

## The read side

Reads that join other aggregates' tables are on `PlanningReads`: `readiness_inputs` (the one five-statement loader), counts, the decision queue's inputs (`draft_goals_of_stories`, `decision_lineage_in`), search, the export readers of items, required decisions, comments and attachments, and `urgency_lineage_in` (the epics and stories an urgent mark reaches), the promise reads `promise_facts_in` and `promised_between_in`, and `item_heads_in` (the level and title of the item at an item link's other end). The export's reads of item links and web links are not among them: `all_item_links_in` is on `ItemLinkRepo` and `all_web_links_in` on `WebLinkRepo`, because nothing else may read these rows ([[general/architecture]], rules 7 and 8). The clock's active and draft sprints (`active_and_draft_in`) read only sprints, so they are on `SprintRepo`. `KeyRepo` holds the one key sequence and every key or external-reference lookup across item types. Reason: a read across aggregates belongs to no single aggregate, and one sequence keeps keys unique across types. `GET /sprints/planning` (`Handlers::sprint_planning`) gives each active and draft goal its discovery and decision gates and their totals, derived on read by `sprint::goal_gates` from the two sprints, their goals and their links read by id (`active_and_draft_in`, then the goals and links), then `initiatives_in`, `epics_in`, `stories_in` and `decisions_in` as whole-table reads, in a fixed number of statements; nothing stores them (kb/ADR-0014 R6), and decision facts are built by `readiness::decision_fact`, as readiness builds them.

Wire DTOs are built only in `handler/dto.rs`; `domain/` returns domain values (`Summary`, `QueueStory`, `QueueEntry`, `DiscoveryRow`, `QueueSummary`; `AffectedStory` is the classifier's input). Reason: the domain must not change when the wire does, and the OpenAPI snapshot catches the wire side. Values are pinned by `crates/reeve-planning/tests/dto_values.rs`.

### The readiness queue

The Readiness page is one list of what holds work back: decisions, and initiatives and epics whose discovery is not done. Nothing in it is stored; it is worked out each time from the stories' readiness, goals and promises (kb/ADR-0014 R6, kb/ADR-0027 R1; `queue::order`).

- **Rows.** An unfinished story whose epic or initiative has no discovery mark is held by the nearest such item: that item is a discovery row. A story is held by one row only. An initiative is a row only for stories whose epic is marked; an epic row whose initiative is unmarked too says so ("with GRA-572"). Stories not started are *held*; started ones are *under way*, and a row holding only those is still a row. The queue reads discovery off readiness, never decides it.
- **Nesting.** An open, unsatisfied decision on a row's own item sits under that row, in the row's lane; every other decision stands alone, and a decision never appears twice.
- **Lanes.**

  | Page lane | Holds | Example line |
  |---|---|---|
  | Needed this sprint | work an active-sprint goal or a promise due this sprint needs | "for goal: Ship tiers" |
  | Needed next sprint | work a draft-sprint goal or a promise due next sprint needs | "promised by 20 Nov: GRA-12" |
  | *No goal or promise needs these yet* | | |
  | Now, no goal yet | rows and decisions with held or under-way work under an initiative in the first horizon bucket | "Now · holds 3" |
  | Later | decisions with affected work anywhere else | |
  | Open, not blocking yet | decisions nothing waits on yet | |
  | Accepted | decisions with an accepted record | |
  | Closed | closed decisions, folded until asked for | |

  Only goals and promises put work in a sprint lane (promises by kb/ADR-0025 R5/R7). A row takes the earliest window of its stories and of the decisions under it. A row that no goal or promise needs and that is not under the first bucket is left out (it would flood Later), and so is an epic without an initiative, unless something gives it a window. With no first bucket configured there is no Now. A record waiting for review stays in its lane; review is a next step, not a lane.
- **Order.** In the two sprint lanes, rows come first (more held stories, then key), each followed by its decisions; then standalone decisions: those holding work first, overdue first, then more held, undated before dated. Later and Open use the same order; Accepted lists the newest check first, Closed the newest close. Rows come first because the discovery is what the decisions wait on. Later never holds a row; Now is ranked as below.
- **The Now ranking.** In Now, by the first rule that differs: (1) urgent (a mark on the item, an ancestor or a descendant); (2) the initiative's target month, earlier first, then none (a malformed month counts as none; the API refuses one a request names, so only an imported or older row holds one); (3) a decision due within a sprint length of today, overdue included (for a row, any decision under it), earliest first; (4) more held stories. On a full tie rows come before decisions.
- **What a row says.** The lane heading says when; the row's line says why: the goal's outcome, the promise date, or the Now rule that placed it: "Now · urgent via GRA-590", "Now · target Nov 2026", "Now · due 17 Nov", "Now · decision #20 due 17 Nov", "Now · holds 3", "Now · holds none, 2 under way". A decision under a row shows no line: its row says why.
- **Urgency only orders Now.** Urgency never moves work into a lane or into a count; it orders Now only. The readiness gate (kb/ADR-0024 R7's "readiness") and moving a story never read the queue, goals or urgency (kb/ADR-0014 R4).
- **Counts.** The summary counts decisions in each lane (those under a row included), records to review in any lane, overdue decisions, and blocking decisions and the stories they hold, each once; the page's summary line shows the records to review and the overdue, since the lane headings carry the rest. The board banner shows only the two window parts, the discovery rows and the decisions needed this sprint and next: "2 in discovery and 1 decision needed this sprint".

## Context map

- **Identity (upstream, authenticates).** Planning's `Ctx { actor, via, now }` is its own type; the server builds it from identity's authenticated token (`crates/reeve-server/src/auth/mod.rs`). Planning never imports identity. Reason: kb/ADR-0010, the supplier can change without planning noticing. Who may write a brief template (an admin) is decided by the server's route from identity's role; planning is not told.
- **Activity (downstream, implements planning's port).** `reeve-activity` implements `ActivitySink`; planning calls `record` in the mutation's transaction.
- **Jira (anticorruption layer in the CLI).** The translation from Jira's status to delivery, type to kind and owner mapping is `crates/reeve/src/jira/`. Planning's import endpoints receive planner-vocabulary items and create them with their state. Planning knows nothing of Jira beyond an opaque external reference.
- **Decision records (outside planning).** Records live in household repositories. The CLI's scheduled check reads them and posts check results; it is the only authority that does (kb/ADR-0004, kb/ADR-0016). Planning stores the reference and the result and never reads a repository. Only an admin's check stores `accepted`.
- **GitHub (informational).** The webhook upserts a story's pull request through `PullRequestRepo` as a cache; status never decides delivery.
